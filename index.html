How we picked your top 3

We scored every business that appeared in the 28 Google Maps searches (the top 20 results each time) by how high it ranked. A #1 earns 10 points, #2 earns 9, down to 1 point for #10; anything below #10 earns nothing. We left out your own listings (including the unrated “Drumm Carpentry Factory” pin), the unrelated Drumm Fencing & Carpentry, and the far-away results Google mixed in from New York, Brooklyn and the UK.

The three highest-scoring local businesses all make or sell fitted kitchens or wardrobes, so they're the three we compare you with. Kitchen World Cork, which you might have expected, came 8th: it's #1 from Cork city but rarely shows up further north.

What to copy

Specific things the three competitors do that you don't yet, each backed by what we found. Only add what's true for your business.

  1. Add secondary categories that fit the work you do

    Sliding Door Wardrobes lists 9 categories, including Furniture maker, Kitchen furniture store and Furniture store, and it leads wardrobe searches from Cork city and Kanturk. You list 4. The businesses at #1 for several “carpenter” searches (for example in Kanturk) use the Carpenter category, which you don't have, even though you have a carpentry services page.

    Evidence: competitor categories from their public Google listings; your categories from your listing, 10 Oct 2026. Categories are one of the main ways Google decides which searches a listing fits.

  2. Give Mallow its own page

    Crafted Fitted Furniture's contact page, which carries its Mallow address, ranks #6 on Google for “fitted kitchens Mallow”, and you're not on the first 2 pages. You have 6 pages aimed at “Cork” but none aimed at Mallow, your home town. Add a “Fitted kitchens & wardrobes in Mallow” page with local projects, and mention nearby Buttevant, Kanturk and Charleville.

    Evidence: Google web search results, Cork location, desktop, 10 Oct 2026; your sitemap.

  3. Add a booking link to your Google listing

    Sliding Door Wardrobes shows “Book online” on its listing, so people can book without phoning. A link to book a showroom visit or a design consultation does the same job.

    Evidence: Sliding Door Wardrobes' public Google listing, 10 Oct 2026.

  4. Fill in accessibility and other listing details

    Sliding Door Wardrobes lists 6 accessibility details (car park, entrance, seating, toilet, hearing loop, assisted listening) and Crafted lists 2. Your listing shows delivery and payment options, but no accessibility details. If your showroom has an accessible entrance or car park, add it.

    Evidence: “About” tab on each public listing; yours from the full listing data.

  5. Win more wardrobe jobs in Cork city and Blarney, and ask for reviews when you do

    Sliding Door Wardrobes, based in Togher, has the Cork city wardrobe searches. Being further away is hard to beat, but reviews and project pages that name Cork city areas help. Ask each Cork city and Blarney customer to review you, and add those jobs to your projects pages under the area name.

    Evidence: Maps searches from Cork city and Blarney, where you weren't in the top 20 for any of the 4 searches.

  6. One easy win none of the four has: LocalBusiness schema

    None of the four websites tells Google its business type, address and hours in structured data (LocalBusiness schema). You already have Organization and FAQ schema, so adding LocalBusiness with your Mallow address and hours is a small job, and it would put you ahead of all three.

    Evidence: structured data found on each site's crawled pages, 10 Oct 2026.

Method and caveats

  • When and how: data from 10 Oct 2026. Google Maps rankings (28 searches), Google web search results (6 searches, Cork location, desktop) and your full listing came from Apify. Competitor listings were checked by hand on Google Maps while signed out, and all four websites were crawled lightly (up to 30 pages each, plus each site's sitemap).
  • Maps results depend on where the searcher is. Each search was run from the centre of one town, zoomed to about 13 km. Someone searching from a different street, or on a phone with location turned on, can see a different order.
  • Proxies aren't traffic. Rankings, review counts, photos and social figures are public signals. They usually move with demand, but they aren't calls, visits or sales.
  • Signed-out view: Google currently shows a “limited view” of Maps when you're signed out. It hides review lists, so competitors' review dates, reply rates and star breakdowns couldn't be read by hand. Rating, categories, photos, attributes, phone, website and hours were visible.
  • Website measures: “service pages” are pages about a product or service (not projects, blog, legal or contact pages), counted from each sitemap. “Town / location pages” have a place name in the address. “LocalBusiness schema” means LocalBusiness or a subtype in the page's structured data.
  • Small samples: one snapshot on one day. Rankings move from day to day, so treat differences of one or two places as noise.
